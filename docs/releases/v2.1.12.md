# Ghost Arcade Pro 2.1.12

## The Timeline

A new **Timeline** tab sits beside the Show tab. The Show tab stays simple: presets and music. The Timeline is the score for the whole stage.

- **One row per mapping layer.** Drop a video, image, shader or sketch on a row and it plays on that piece for the block's span. Click a block to edit it in the normal Properties and Shader panels. A row with no blocks shows the piece's own look; a row with blocks hides its piece between them. Add layers to a piece as sub-rows that draw over it, with their own blend.
- **Group rows.** + Group row: pick pieces on the canvas and name the group (Left half, Top row, All). Its blocks play across those pieces as one picture, or the whole picture on each piece. Edit pieces changes the group for every block on the row.
- **Presets row.** Each block loads a saved preset at its start, with its transition. Drop a card from the Presets tray, or choose one with +.
- **Looks row.** Drag a Look from the Looks gallery onto the Timeline and the shapes wear it for the block's span.
- **Edit blocks the way a video editor does.** Move, trim, split, crossfade, copy-drag (Alt), ripple delete (Shift+Delete), undo and redo, snapping to the beat grid, block edges, the playhead and markers.
- **Control lines.** Any setting of any layer can have a line: opacity, blend, visibility, corner pins, mesh points, move, scale and rotate, shader and effect settings, sketch controls, video speed. Draw a line with the pen or with points and curves, let it follow the music (level, bass, kick and more), an LFO, a MIDI knob, an OSC address or an Ableton knob, or key it live. Keys can be selected, shifted, copied and deleted like keyframes.
- **Where lines** move a look or a block's content across the pieces in an order you choose. Pick a block's pieces right on the canvas. Double-click a block's pattern to edit it as a step grid, with a new Steps motion (4 to 32 steps, with Snake, Every other, Random and Clear fills).
- **LED rows** drive WLED controllers: colors, gradients, chases, effects, or following the projected picture.
- **Screen FX and Effects rows** run stage effects and composition effects for a span.
- **Audio rows.** **Add audio** puts a song or soundtrack on the Timeline; its tempo becomes the grid. Audio rows have level, fades, ducking and effects, and are heard in Render to video. An **Ableton clips row** fires clips in Ableton Live.
- **Sections and GO.** Name the parts of a song. A section can play on, hold for GO, loop until GO, jump back after a number of passes, or hand the stage to the VJ decks until GO. Sections can come from Live's locators and be sent back to them.
- **Phrases** launch a section as an audio and visual phrase on the next bar, from pads in VJ mode or on the Timeline, firing Live clips and scenes ahead of time.
- **Auto show.** Add audio, press ✦ Auto show, and the Timeline builds a whole show on your existing mapping: piece groups, content from the built-in catalog or your library, looks, Screen FX and builds that follow the song's sections and style. Shuffle until you like it; one undo takes it back.
- **Follow the clock.** The Timeline follows MIDI clock, Ableton Link, Live's song position and timecode, with a tempo map and beat grid of its own. Bar launches follow the show's meter (3/4, 6/8), not only 4/4.
- **Live takeover.** Shift+L (or take over on a row) hands a row to a VJ deck row, the mix, a group, dropped media or black; R hands everything back, now or on the next beat, bar, phrase or marker.

## A calmer Timeline layout

- **One slim toolbar** in three groups: transport, editing tools (shown once there are blocks) and output. The **?** menu holds the keyboard shortcuts, a Timeline tour, hover help for each area (F1) and the online guide.
- **A side column** with **Inspector**, **Media** and **Perform** tabs. Clicking a row opens the Media tab with that row as the target; clicking a card puts it on the row at the playhead.
- **Rows in folding sections:** Groups, Layers, Audio, Stage & FX and Control. Rows show up once they are used; **+ Row** lists every kind with a one-line description, and a pin shows every row, empty ones too. The piece rows fold under one **Layers** header.
- **Sections** run as a band under the ruler, and **markers** are numbered cue flags (M adds one).
- An empty Timeline says what to do first, and Play works on an empty Timeline.
- **Room to work** (the ` key with the pointer over the Timeline) gives the Timeline the whole window.
- The Output button holds the Timeline's mute, level and sound card.

## Perform

The **Perform** tab runs the show from one place.

- **One GO** for everything: sections that hold or loop, hand-offs to the VJ decks, markers and manual cues. Enter, the toolbar GO, the Perform GO and MIDI or OSC all do the same thing.
- **Back, Stop and Reset** beside GO, and **one cue list** of sections, markers and cues, numbered once, with what is playing now and what is standing by.
- **Pads and Phrases** in the same tab, and the takeover controls: Return mode, fade, and Return (R).

## Live key

**Live key** (it was called Record) writes what you do onto the Timeline, so it is not mixed up with recording the output.

- Turn on **Live key** and play: everything you change in Mapping, the panels or with a controller is keyed onto the Timeline as you do it. Settings become lines, one undo step per gesture; putting other content on a piece becomes a block on its row.
- Stopped, changes are held and show on the stage at once. **Update** keys them all where they were made, as one undo step; Discard puts them back.
- **Live key options:** key lines live, Touch or Latch, follow edits, and bind a knob to a line.
- With Live key on, Play starts on an empty Timeline and the playhead rolls on past the end, so there is always time to key into. A take can be 30 minutes at most.
- Engines that move values on their own (audio and LFO modulation, Auto, Music motion, sections, cues and takeovers) are never keyed as your edits.

## Keys a video editor already knows

- **J K L** play backwards (press J again for 2×, 4×, 8×), stop and play.
- **I** and **O** set in and out points, drawn on the ruler and saved with the Timeline; with Loop on, play loops between them. Shift+I / Shift+O go to them, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+X clears them.
- **↑ ↓** jump to the previous or next edit point; **← →** step a frame when nothing is selected.
- Tools for blocks: **Select** (V), **Razor** (C), **Ripple** (Shift+B: every later block on the row moves with the edge; B stays Blackout), **Roll** (N) and **Slip** (Y). Ctrl/Cmd+K splits at the playhead.
- **Ctrl/Cmd+G** groups blocks across rows so they select, move and copy as one; **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+G** ungroups.
- Control lines take **Bezier** curves: right-click a point, choose Bezier, and drag its handles.
- **Q** and **W** trim the block under the playhead to it. **= - \\** zoom in, out and to fit. **U** shows or hides a row's lines. **S** toggles snapping.
- The playhead line runs down every row, however far the rows are scrolled. All of these keys are in the shortcut sheet.

## Render to video

**File → Render to video…** now makes every frame on its own clock, so the result does not depend on how fast the computer is.

- **Exact frame rates** from 23.976 to **120 fps** (23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50, 59.94, 60, 120). A video on a row shows exactly the right frame at any rate, never skipping or showing one early.
- **The sound in sync.** The Timeline's audio rows are rendered as you hear them live (level, mute, fades, ducking, effects, lines) and stay within one frame of the picture over the whole render.
- **three.js and p5.js pages are stepped frame by frame**, so their motion, timers and music reactions match the render. A page that must run on its own clock can opt out with `<meta name="ghost-arcade-clock" content="live">`.
- Shaders, effects, Auto, modulation, Music motion, Screen FX, fades and Interactive scenes all move exactly one frame per frame. Simulations start fresh, so the same show renders the same way twice.
- **Length:** the whole Timeline or show, in to out, or a number of seconds from the start.
- **Quality per format:** H.264 Smaller file, High or Master; ProRes 422 LT, 422 HQ or 4444 XQ; HAP or HAP Q. The dialog estimates the file size and how long the render will take.
- **Dark frames are kept.** A show that starts, holds or ends on black renders as it should. A video that stops moving in a render stops it with a message naming the file.
- Renders that play videos are much faster, long soundtracks use far less memory, and a render's temporary files are cleaned up when it ends, is cancelled or the app quits.

## Rehearse and record the stage

- Stage Sim plays the Timeline as a rehearsal, records it with sound and an orbiting or shot-based camera, and renders a rehearsal video. Recordings capture a new picture every frame, so short flashes are kept. It asks where to save, and asks before closing in the middle of a recording.
- A stage-camera lane on the Timeline cuts between Demo Reel shots.
- Demo Reel renders from the stage view, with no grey venue shapes over the content, and asks where to save when it finishes.

## Running unattended

- **Install mode** runs a show or a Timeline unattended: a schedule, a watchdog that restarts what stops, resume after a restart, a health log, and WLED controllers reconnected.
- Shader time, flicker, grain and glitch effects keep their look on installs that run for days.

## Interactive Studio and 3D worlds

- **Add layer → Interactive 3D** builds a world from the stage: each mapped shape stands up as a lit block with shadows, and the Interactive Studio's **3D World** tab edits it while it plays.
- Blocks take a finish: stone, matte, chrome, glass, neon, ink, or a real object that only takes light and shadow. **Orbit** turns the camera round the blocks, with the output following.
- **Balls** bounce down the blocks, **paint** runs down them (**Pour**), and **fire and smoke** burn on them (**Ignite**). Four new starters: Cascade, Fire wall, Paint gallery and Marbles.
- **Trigger zones** fire show actions when something comes into them: a Look, a preset, a VJ clip or column, a section, GO, a cue, a phrase, a Live clip or scene, blackout or the transport, with a re-arm time and a Test button. Zones can also set the world on fire, pour paint or drop balls.
- Tracked **hands and body** from the hand tracking camera move the effects and touch zones.
- **Real objects:** the 3D World's Shape switch picks a flat wall or real objects. Setup walks through the faces (from your corner-mapped layers), what each face is (Top, Side or Back), which way is down and where the projector is. **Effects only** draws just the light, paint and shadows, for projecting onto the objects themselves, and a backdrop catches what the projector throws past them.

## Phone

- **Phone camera in VJ Sources.** A paired phone's camera and depth views (Mobile Dual Camera, Mobile Depth Map, Mobile Depth Contours, Mobile Point Cloud and more) appear by name in the Sources tab. Place them in deck cells, trigger them and see them on the output like any live source. Each shows Connecting…, Live or No frames. The phone only says it is live once the desktop shows its picture.
- When a phone goes away, its cells say so and the output shows nothing rather than a frozen frame. When it comes back, the same cells light up again with no re-placing.
- **Room scan from a phone.** With real objects in the 3D World, **Scan with the phone** asks a paired iPhone with LiDAR to scan the room, and the room is built from the scan. A phone can also offer a scan on its own; the desktop asks first, or always accepts from paired phones if you choose. Scans are kept in the Media Library's Scans tab. Large scans are thinned evenly, not cut off.
- **Phone world.** A phone editing an Interactive scene can send its 3D world, tools and zones, and its Test and Orbit act on the desktop. Trigger zones made on earlier versions of the phone app now fire.

## Mapping

- **Link to surface…** in the Geometry panel (and on a preset's right-click menu) links a layer in chosen presets to an existing shared surface, so one warp edit reaches all of them again. It shows each preset and layer, asks you to settle any conflicts, and lists what will happen before it applies. One undo takes it back. Media, effects and routing in each preset stay as they were.
- A duplicated layer no longer takes over the original's shared warp when it is shared again.

## Dome

- **Inside view:** see the dome as someone at its center does, with the layers placed on it and the grid. Drag to look around, scroll to zoom; Front and Up jump to the usual views. A room with seats and a console can be shown round it, with Wide and Seat sliders.
- **Place layers on the dome** from the Inside view, and a **dome grid** on every output.
- The canvas can be a **Panorama** that wraps round the dome (a true 360 picture meets itself without a line), a **Domemaster**, or a **Flat view**. **Seam blend** crossfades the ends of content not made to wrap. Older projects keep their look.
- **Projectors around the dome:** the Dome output panel lays out a ring of 2 to 7 projectors plus one for the cap, each a Screen with its own **Dome projector** settings, blended where they overlap. Their footprints show on the dome picture and the Inside view.
- **Mirror domes:** a Screen can import a mirror-dome warp mesh (text or binary) for a single projector bouncing off a spherical mirror.

## Video and HAP

- HAP clips with a start delay now play on the GPU instead of falling back to software.
- The video badge names a HAP variant that is not supported (HAP Q Alpha, HAP Alpha-Only, HAP R, HAP HDR) and says what to do: convert it with the built-in converter, or export it again as HAP, HAP Alpha or HAP Q. Non-square pixels and rotated files say so too.

## Music motion

- **Music motion** on a shader layer or clip moves the shader's own time with the music: slow in quiet parts, quicker as it builds, a push on each hit. Eight Featured shaders are made for it and carry a small waveform mark. When it is on, the button is solid blue and the VJ cell gets a blue ring.

## Find latest

## Stability and speed

- **Editor crash recovery.** If the editor crashes or stops answering, it is reloaded up to three times, and the app stays open. If that does not help, a message says why, with **Reload editor**, **Show log file** and **Quit**. A window that starts answering again is never reloaded.
- The log records why the editor or the graphics process stopped, the graphics card and driver in use, and a lost graphics device. A log that another program is holding is added to, not wiped.
- Clip sound plays at audio priority, so a busy computer no longer plays gaps.
- Moving faders, Auto, modulation, Music motion, Screen FX, crossfades and live input no longer rebuild the scene every frame, so the app uses much less CPU while a show plays. The Timeline redraws only what changes.
- Shaders shown on many pieces at once are rendered once for the group.
- Temporary folders from renders, recordings and clip sound are removed when they are done and swept at the next launch.

## Look and layout

- The interface's 100% is now what 90% was, so the editor fits a laptop screen; a size you saved looks the same as before.
- The app window opens maximized.
- The VJ top bar keeps each side in its own space, folding Preview, Stage Sim and Stage Edit to icons on a narrow window.
- **Make a show to a song** leaves the welcome screen; building a show from a song stays in the File menu.
- American spelling throughout, and every new word is translated into Spanish, German, French, Brazilian Portuguese, Italian and Japanese.

## Fixes

- On Windows laptops with two graphics cards, cameras and screen capture show their picture instead of staying blank. A camera or screen capture the app cannot open says why, and a source says Live only once its picture is on screen.
- On Windows, the app behind the editor no longer shows through it when another window is raised without focus.
- MIDI learn listens to every connected input, so a keyboard that is not set as a controller can teach a control. Learn boxes stay put so clicks land, and the overlay sits above the Settings dialog.
- Song analysis finds the same tempo at every sample rate, no longer reads a kick on every beat at the wrong tempo, and checks for double and half tempo.
- Glitch no longer goes still a few minutes after launch, and its Speed and Block hold controls do what they say. New Block Corrupt and Data Bend effects.
- Effects on VJ groups hear the music, and audio-driven effects on instrument layers follow the music again.
- Shader clips keep their own time when the render core restarts.
- Synced LFOs stay in range before bar 1.
- Auto show no longer freezes on "Listening to the song", and closing its dialog stops reading the song.
